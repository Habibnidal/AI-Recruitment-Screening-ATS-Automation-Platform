# Business Challenge

## Context

A specialised recruitment agency processes high volumes of inbound candidate applications across multiple open roles. Candidates apply via an Applicant Tracking System (ATS), which delivers basic application metadata, a CV file, and structured profile information.

The agency's value to clients depends on speed and quality of shortlisting. Recruiters need to evaluate every applicant against client-specific criteria, verify credentials, check prior contact history, and surface the top candidates for interview — all within a tight window before competitors do the same.

## The Manual Process (Before)

For every new application, a recruiter executed roughly the following steps by hand:

1. **Email triage** — Open inbox, find ATS notification email
2. **Open the candidate in the ATS** — Navigate to candidate profile
3. **Download CV** — Click through to download the PDF/DOCX
4. **File the CV** — Navigate to Google Drive, find or create the correct job folder, upload the file, rename consistently
5. **Read the CV** — Skim qualifications, experience, location
6. **Write a brief summary** — Note the candidate's profile in shorthand
7. **Score the candidate** — Apply mental scoring rules (years of experience, country, certifications, qualifications)
8. **Check for duplicates** — Search the master tracking sheet by email to see if this person has applied before
9. **Check recruiter outreach history** — Search 3 separate recruiter inboxes (each recruiter has their own) to see if the candidate has been emailed previously by anyone on the team
10. **Log the candidate** — Add a row to the master tracking sheet with all the above fields populated correctly

### Time per candidate
- **Average:** 10-12 minutes
- **Best case:** 7-8 minutes (familiar role, clear CV)
- **Worst case:** 15-20 minutes (ambiguous CV, complex job title, multi-tab cross-checking)

### Volume
- **Steady state:** 30-50 applications/day across active jobs
- **Peak:** 60-80 applications/day during active campaigns
- **Real ceiling:** ~25 candidates/day per recruiter could be properly processed

## The Pain Points

### 1. Recruiter time was being burned on data entry
Senior recruiters were spending 4-6 hours/day on intake instead of interviewing candidates or having conversations with clients. The skill gap between "filing a CV" and "evaluating a senior hire" is enormous, but both tasks consumed similar amounts of their day.

### 2. Inconsistency in scoring
Different recruiters scored the same candidate differently. Some weighted experience more heavily, some prioritised location, some ignored certain certifications. There was a written scoring rubric, but human application of it was inconsistent.

### 3. Duplicate contact errors
Candidates were occasionally emailed by two different recruiters within days of each other because the prior contact wasn't surfaced during triage. This is embarrassing professionally and burns goodwill with candidates.

### 4. Lost applications
Some candidates simply never made it to the tracking sheet because the recruiter who received the ATS notification was on leave, or the email was missed in a busy inbox. Lost candidates = lost revenue.

### 5. No audit trail
There was no consistent log of "when did we receive X, when did we contact them, what score did we give." When clients asked "why didn't you submit candidate Y," nobody could answer with certainty.

### 6. Folder structure drift
Every recruiter created Drive folders slightly differently — `Cardiologist Dubai`, `Dubai Cardiologist`, `cardiologist - dubai`, etc. The Drive structure became a mess, making it impossible to find historical CVs for a role.

### 7. The volume ceiling
Because the process was manual, the business literally could not grow past a certain volume without hiring more recruiters. Each new client/role added more daily intake work, not more sales-driving work.

## The Strategic Cost

Beyond the operational pain, there was a strategic cost: **the business was incentivised to be less aggressive in pursuing new clients** because each new client meant more intake load. Growth was capped by an administrative bottleneck.

The agency leadership identified intake automation as the highest-leverage internal investment available — every minute removed from the per-candidate workflow could be redirected toward client-facing activity.

## Why Automation Was the Answer

The intake process had three characteristics that made it ideal for automation:

1. **High volume** — Daily, repetitive, predictable structure
2. **Rule-based** — Scoring, duplicate detection, folder filing all follow defined rules
3. **Multi-system coordination** — Most steps involve copy/pasting between systems, which automation eliminates

The interesting analytical part — *should we actually pursue this candidate?* — is judgment work, not automation work. Automation could remove the data-entry tax so recruiters spent more time on judgment.

## Success Criteria

When commissioning the automation, the business defined success as:

- ✅ Every application gets processed within 5 minutes of submission
- ✅ Every candidate has a complete tracking sheet row, every time
- ✅ Duplicate detection runs automatically and accurately
- ✅ Drive structure is consistent and self-maintaining
- ✅ Scoring is deterministic and explainable
- ✅ Audit trail captures every action
- ✅ Recruiters can spend at least 50% more time on candidate-facing work

All of these were achieved.

## Beyond Time Savings

The unexpected benefit: **the platform changed what the business was willing to take on**. Because new client onboarding now adds zero intake load, the agency can pursue more accounts, larger contracts, and more diverse role types without scaling the recruiting team proportionally.

This is the leverage that intake automation unlocks — not just "faster processing" but "freedom to grow."
